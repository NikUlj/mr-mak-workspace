# Project Playground

A new game project comparing third-person survival horror, a mech action
roguelite, and an incremental game. The owner's clarified leading priority is
earning potential, with production feasibility to be tested in a prototype.
Project Playground is the working name; the final game title is undecided.

The [project brief](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/brief.md)
records the candidates and known constraints. The
[research plan](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/research-plan.md)
defines the comparison; [initial evidence](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/initial-evidence.md)
contains the first source checks. No production direction has been chosen.

The owner can work approximately 21 hours/week now, with 31 hours/week possible
later, and plans to create all assets personally through AI-assisted workflows.
The likely model generator is Tripo; animation is planned through a GPT agent
using Blender MCP and video references, with manual fixes in Cascadeur or Blender.
The goal is an early release with strong financial returns.
Both 3D candidates should use a simpler low-poly aesthetic. The owner prefers
the mech idea, with horror a close second, and has no previous commercial game
releases or existing launch audience. The
[Step 1 findings](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/constraints-and-reuse.md)
record the research baseline and conditional reuse of existing work. The
[Step 2 game scopes](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/game-scopes.md)
now reflect the owner's correction that the first horror and mech scopes were
too small. The target is a 4-6-hour horror campaign in one substantial
interconnected location, and a mech game with both deeper customization and
more areas, enemies, and bosses. Exact counts remain proposed costing inputs;
the smaller versions are fallback scenarios. Scrapline remains a provisional
incremental candidate. Early Access and episodic releases are confirmed options
for the larger games, alongside a full launch. Compare time to first paid release,
time to completion, support workload, and staged revenue without assuming a
release strategy has been chosen. The
[Step 3 market findings](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/market-findings.md)
now cover 29 paid releases, 99 player reviews and staged-release histories.
The mech remains a serious candidate; no earnings winner is established.
The [Step 4 asset findings](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/asset-findings.md)
now expand all three scopes into 69 asset/content rows with provisional owner-hour
ranges, staged-release allocations and AI-tool credit scenarios. Horror and mech
have similar uncalibrated asset workloads; the schematic incremental is smaller.
The [production pipeline and proof plan](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/production-pipeline.md)
specifies how to measure the decisive uncertainties. No timed benchmark or paid
generation has been performed. [Step 5 production estimates](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/production-estimates.md)
and [commercial findings](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/commercial-findings.md)
now add 55 work/risk packages, staged support, 21/31-hour schedules and editable
financial scenarios. The owner confirmed **EUR**; the spending cap remains
undecided. Original exploratory middle full-release estimates are approximately
3,400 horror, 4,000 mech and 960 Scrapline hours before launch, all unmeasured and
now under review. Equal-volume sales
tests establish neither expected sales nor a profit winner. The
[scenario model](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/scenario-model.html)
supports changes to workload, hours, sales, prices, deductions and costs.
The owner used the controller task as a plausibility check on the whole model,
finding its range excessive even for fresh implementation. The
[estimate review](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/estimate-review.md)
records that the objection is about estimate credibility, not merely prototype
reuse. The original values remain exploratory; no calibrated replacements have
been established. On 23 October the owner agreed to assess commercial opportunity,
choose a representative prototype, then rebuild estimates using the observations
before making a production commitment. A full row-by-row repair is no longer a
prerequisite for this provisional decision.

The [prototype decision](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/prototype-decision.md)
recommends testing the mech first, horror second, with the provisional incremental
retained as a concept alternative. This is the agent's test-order recommendation,
not an established profit ranking or the owner's final game selection. No new
prototype has been implemented or measured in this research revision.

Existing Unity prototypes remain in `F:\Unity Projects\Dead Space` and
`F:\Unity Projects\AC_Game`. The [file and reuse audit](../../workspace/2026-10-21_project-playground/prototype-audit.md)
records the starting state; runtime quality has not been checked. The identified
mech competitor is RIG Riot.
