# Story attributes

Working brief for Mech Prototype, 17 November 2026. **The owner's preferred starting point for exploration is the pact through which each patron lends the player a distinct mech.** The setting, opposition and player's reason for seeking that pact are open.

## The experience of entering the world

- **Recognition followed by discovery.** The openings of *Demon’s Souls* and *Dark Souls* are touchstones. A player should understand a familiar idea well enough to be surprised by what it means here. Hearing something immense pray to you works because you already understand what it means to pray.
- **A central idea with symbolic depth.** Fire in *Dark Souls* is the example the owner gave. A player can grasp what is happening to the fire and still have a personal interpretation of what it means to keep it burning. The symbol, its meaning and whether it takes physical form are open.
- **A faint sense of loss.** The former world can be well understood in the design notes and barely glimpsed by the player. One detail that gives the present situation emotional weight may be enough. Give direct exposition about former daily life little space.
- **Enough certainty to act.** Give the player a legible predicament, something they want, and a reason to fight. The cause of the catastrophe, the nature of a patron and the full consequences of succeeding can stay obscure. *Elden Ring* is also a reference for that room to interpret.
- **An impersonal register.** Let places, beliefs and actions carry the emotion. Avoid invented family memories and intimate backstory for the pilot. An exaggerated fairy tale is one optional way to introduce a world.

## Patrons, machines and opposition

- **An optional visual influence.** *Warhammer 40,000* came up for its grimdark, religious aesthetic. The owner is also interested in occult rites that might create, awaken or enable a mech. Each idea can draw on either influence when it fits.
- **Distinct patrons and a meaningful bargain.** The direction the owner wants to explore is a choice among powerful, possibly demon-like patrons. Each lends the player a complete chassis under some form of contract. The player needs a reason to seek the mech and a consequence for accepting it. What the patron ultimately gains can stay mysterious.
- **An intelligence that feels unfamiliar.** People might have good reason to use a word like *demon* even if the being looks and behaves unlike a conventional demon. Short poetic speech appeals to the owner. Its words might make a request intelligible while leaving its motives difficult to read. Being prayed to by something immense was an especially strong response to *The Hand*. How often this happens, or whether another patron speaks at all, is open.
- **Opponents worth imagining and fighting.** A premise should suggest what the opposition wants, why it clashes with the player, and an enemy whose shape or movement makes the encounter distinctive. The player can understand what the enemy is doing well before learning what it is.

## Where the story meets the game

The agreed game direction is an *Armored Core*-inspired roguelike or roguelite. The current preference is for several distinctive playable chassis with access to the same weapons, modules and other run equipment. A setting should suggest why someone would take one of these bodies into danger and what a run might accomplish. The pilot’s identity, the makers of the mechs, the terms of a pact, run structure and persistence are all open. A practical test for a premise is whether it suggests one specific mech the owner would actually want to model, rig, animate and play. [Project decision log](#/mech-prototype/3)

## Comparing premises

Look for differences in what the player wants, what puts them in conflict with others, and how the pact changes their position. A new landscape or enemy can still leave the pilot playing the same part as in [The Hand](#/mech-world-concepts/0).

## What should the enemies be like?

The owner wants a practical limit on the time it takes to make ordinary enemies before developing another setting. Biological opponents appeal, simple mechanical units look economical, and cyborgs and a low-poly PSX aesthetic are possibilities. Bosses and other exceptional enemies can be considered separately.

**Working proposal for common enemies.** Favor bodies that can move convincingly with few animated parts. A wheeled carriage, hovering hull or planted body can share most of its movement across several enemies. Spend the distinctive animation on an action the player notices, such as a body swelling before it fires. This lets later settings use mechanical, living or grafted opponents. The actual asset effort is still untested.

Consider a ranged enemy that moves sideways, pauses and fires. A hovering gun platform needs a rigid hull, a pivoting mount and a recoil motion. A floating organism could have a hard shell and a throat sac that inflates as it takes aim and collapses when it spits. A cyborg could carry that same organ in an open cradle on wheels. The cradle handles travel and turning, while the pulse of the organ signals both life and the timing of the shot. The hybrid adds a second material and an attachment to model. Its advantage is clearest when that living part also does a job in combat.

The proposed short-legged charger is still an option for a more involved enemy. Its hard turn requires planted feet and a shift in weight, whether its legs are fleshy or plated. A low-poly style can make held poses and abrupt recoils expressive. Sliding through a planted foot or firing before a wind-up reads will still affect the encounter. When comparing future candidates, consider the work to model and rig one enemy and how much of that work a second enemy could reuse.

## What does the player want from the patron?

What does the player want before any patron offers a chassis? Why would the mech make that goal possible? Who has reason to oppose the pilot, and what does accepting the loan cost even if the pilot succeeds? The player can understand those stakes while the patron’s own purpose remains mysterious.
