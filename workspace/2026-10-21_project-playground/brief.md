# Project Playground

A space to discover and develop a new game. Three directions are under
consideration; none has been selected for production.

## Starting point

- **Purpose:** Choose a game with a strong commercial opportunity, then test its
  appeal and production workflow before committing to the completed scope.
- **Stage:** Steps 1–5 desk research documented, including market evidence,
  asset/content inventories, full production estimates and editable EUR financial
  scenarios. Native prototype validation and timed production benchmarks remain
  pending. Step 5's hours remain disputed. The owner agreed on 23 October to
  choose a prototype on commercial grounds before rebuilding the estimates;
  the [provisional recommendation](prototype-decision.md) is available.
- **Working name:** Project Playground. The final game title can come later.

## The owner's priorities

- Programming is a stronger skill than making 3D assets and animations.
- The amount and difficulty of asset creation are especially important.
- Both 3D games should use a **simpler, low-poly aesthetic**.
- The initial horror and mech proposals were too small. Target a **4-6-hour
  horror campaign in one substantial interconnected location**, and a mech game
  with **both deeper customization and more areas, enemies, and bosses**.
  Exact content quantities and feature choices remain research proposals.
- **Early Access and episodic releases are acceptable options** for the larger
  games. Compare them with a full launch; no release route or payment structure
  has been selected. Separate the first paid release from completing the game.
- Personal preference is the **mech game**, with **horror a close second**.
- There are **no previous commercial releases or existing launch audience**.
- Compare development time, required assets, and profitability.
- **Priority clarified, 23 October:** Earning potential leads the next decision.
  Assess the commercial opportunity first, obtain representative prototype
  evidence, then revise the effort model. The owner need not estimate unfamiliar
  tasks; the original low estimates have not been adopted as corrected values.
- Available development time is approximately **21 hours per week now**, rising
  to **31 hours per week later**; the changeover date is not specified.
- Release as soon as practical and seek the strongest financial outcome. No
  fixed deadline or minimum income target has been supplied.
- Create all assets personally with AI-assisted 3D generation and animation
  workflows. Outsourcing and purchased art packs are not the production baseline.
- Likely modeling tool: **Tripo**, after trying several alternatives.
- Intended animation workflow: a **GPT agent using Blender MCP and a video
  reference**, followed by the owner's corrections in **Cascadeur or Blender**.
- Consider other ideas if they offer a better fit, while keeping the three
  candidates above as the main focus.

## Estimate credibility review

The owner used the controller task, whose duration they understand, to question
the credibility of the wider hour model even for fresh implementation. The
[estimate review](estimate-review.md) records that correction. Original numerical
scenarios remain reproducible, but are not accepted schedules; no calibrated
replacement rates have been established. Do not use those durations or
earnings-per-hour comparisons to choose a production game. The agreed sequence
now permits a commercial recommendation for the first prototype before that
rebuild. Prototype observations should inform the later estimates.

## Provisional prototype decision

The [commercial/prototype memo](prototype-decision.md) recommends testing the
mech first, with horror as runner-up and the provisional incremental retained
as an alternative requiring a stronger concept. This is a test priority, not
an established earnings ranking or the owner's production selection. The
larger intended games remain the release targets; a small test does not shrink
them. Prototype implementation and measured results remain outstanding.
