02 / Current systems

What the source already contains.

Existing implementation is a starting point for review, rather than a final design commitment.

MovementSource present

Move, jump, dash

Camera-relative movement with ground and air acceleration, friction, gravity, capsule-cast collision sliding, and step handling. The model faces camera-forward.

Assets/Scripts/PlayerController.cs
Camera & targetingSource present

Automatic aim selection

Cinemachine third-person following and a crosshair-based target search with a visibility raycast. The configured targeting range is 20 units, with a radius of 6.5% of screen height. The crosshair changes color when a target is selected.

Assets/Scripts/LockOnAim.cs
WeaponsSource present

Held fire & pooled bullets

Attack fires while held, using a 0.25-second cooldown and either a selected target or the camera crosshair ray. Bullet pools start at 30 per type and expand as needed.

ShootingController.cs · BulletManager.cs
Projectile dataConfigured

One default bullet type

The default asset sets damage to 10, speed to 20 units/second, and lifetime to 30 seconds. Gravity and piercing are disabled; impact effects and audio are unassigned.

Assets/Scriptable Objects/DefaultBulletType.asset
Scene connections

On Main Camera, the Cinemachine Brain Updated event calls PlayerAimAndFire.OnCameraUpdated. Locked shots lead the selected hitbox. Free aim follows the reticle. Both weapons use the scene projectile pool and each actor owns its health. The HUD shows both HP values and colors the reticle when locked.

Stationary opponent

The target starts 14.2 units from the player. Its amber cue lasts 0.75 seconds, followed by one shot and 1 second of recovery. The target starts on Direct. Switch its Shooting Controller’s Aim Mode to Predictive to compare the same attack. Step 4 adds win, loss and retry.

05 / Review notes

What has been verified?

Keep file inspection distinct from tested game behavior.

25 November 2026 · Step 3 automated checks

Unity 6000.5.2f1 passed 11 Edit Mode and 17 Play Mode cases on a temporary copy of the saved project. A locked player shot hit a crossing target. Against the enemy, a steady strafe avoided direct aim and took damage from predictive aim. Reversing after the predictive cue avoided the shot. At the scene spawn, standing still cost 10 HP and a keyboard dash preserved all 30 HP.

Cover blocked acquisition and stopped a previously committed shot. Both guns fired on the same frame and a recycled player round passed the enemy’s own hitbox and damaged the player. Killing the enemy during the next wind-up immediately cleared the cue and canceled its shot. A hands-on comparison of cue visibility and dodge timing is still open.

25 November 2026 · Step 2 hands-on playtest

The owner completed the playtest and reported that everything seemed to work fine. This closes the damageable-target checkpoint. Step 3 can use the current movement and targeting settings to test a stationary opponent’s return shot.

24 November 2026 · Turning and firing on the same frame

Two Play Mode cases turn the virtual camera on the frame Attack is pressed. The scene’s Cinemachine Brain applies the pose. With the target inside the ring and off the center dot, the projectile leaves the muzzle aimed at the target. Turning away releases lock and sends the round toward the new center ray. Both cases checked the actual projectile direction on the press frame. The six earlier shooting cases also passed with the Brain active.

Reversing the refresh and fire calls in the isolated test copy made both direction checks fail. Restoring the intended order made both pass. The hands-on question is whether the ring and shot feel aligned during a fast strafe or dash.

23 November 2026 · Damageable target

In Unity 6000.5.2f1, one Edit Mode test verified overkill, ignored nonpositive damage and a single death notification. Six Play Mode tests used an isolated copy of Prototype Map. Held mouse fire took the offset child hitbox through 30, 20, 10 and 0 HP, and the ring returned to its unlocked color. Free aim hit beyond lock range. A wall in the camera’s sightline broke lock, while a wall that blocked only the muzzle stopped the round and preserved all 30 HP.

The other checks covered the four ring edges at 1280 × 720 and 800 × 600, fire crossing another pooled shot and the shooter’s own collider, and a reused round hitting its previous owner for the new damage amount. The human playtest question is how tracking feels while moving and dashing.

22 November 2026 · Movement baseline and lock radius

The owner reported that the movement, camera and firing tests worked with ground speed 15 and dash impulse 50. The player prefab records both values in ab5d1fa. The owner also raised three playtest questions. Does the ring accurately predict lock? Would a cooldown improve dash timing? Would holding Space to climb give the player another useful way to dodge?

The lock query uses 6.5% of screen height. The HUD scales its 100-unit square ring with screen height against a 720-pixel reference. At that height the threshold is 46.8 pixels from center. Measuring the assigned sprite along both axes puts the visible stroke at roughly 44–47 pixels. A saved player check also acquired the centered-pivot target at 6% and released it at 7.5% of screen height at 640 × 360, 1920 × 1080 and 800 × 600.

A focused Play Mode check in a temporary copy of ab5d1fa confirmed lock one pixel inside the cutoff and loss of lock one pixel outside on all four sides at 1280 × 720 and 800 × 600. The cutoffs were 46.8 and 39 pixels. Moving the target’s pivot sideways while leaving its unobstructed collider centered under the reticle broke lock. The selector checks the collider’s bounds center on screen, then checks visibility toward the pivot. The first step 2 fix is to use the same target point for both checks and for locked shots.

20 November 2026 · Uphill stutter

The player gained height in bursts on a 5.7° ramp. At a steady 20 m/s and 60 movement updates per second, it advanced 33.3 cm every update, stayed grounded, and repeatedly rose 0, 0, then 10 cm. The ramp rises 3.3 cm over each of those horizontal increments.

Before the fix, PlayerController.GroundMove chose the direct movement path whenever it traveled as far horizontally as the raised path. As the ground rose into the capsule’s 7.5 cm clearance, hitDown.distance > skinWidth skipped the small upward correction. When the direct path finally hit the slope and lost distance, the raised path restored all the missing height in one update.

An isolated Unity 6000.5.2f1 physics run used the map’s collision mesh, the working controller’s movement methods (source blob a68a86c), the player prefab settings, and fixed forward input. Accepting a positive ground-hit distance on either side of the desired clearance gave steady rises of 6.7, 3.3 and 1.7 cm at 30, 60 and 120 updates per second respectively. Changing only the slide normal left the 60 Hz pattern intact.

The early Dead Space controller at 12861c0 has the same two-way clearance correction. A later revision, 2cbd04c, switched to Unity’s built-in controller.

The fix is in AC_Game/Assets/Scripts/PlayerController.cs. After a positive ground hit, the controller restores clearance in either direction and limits upward corrections to the space beneath a ceiling.

Play Mode verification. Four automated checks passed in a temporary copy of the Prototype Map scene with the edited controller and player prefab. The player climbed and descended the 5.7° ramp evenly at 30, 60 and 120 updates per second. At 60, each uphill update gained 3.33 cm. The player also crossed a 25 cm step in both height directions, respected a low ceiling on the ramp, and jumped and landed on flat ground.

20 November 2026 · Targeting and cover

A saved Play Mode test from a temporary project copy passed with the current aiming scripts and bullet prefab. Three pooled projectiles between camera and target preserved the lock. A wall broke the lock, free aim pointed at the wall, and removing the wall restored the lock. Moving a projectile back to the Default layer reproduced the obstruction.

This check inspected the initial direction of locked and free shots. The later resize check is recorded above. The damageable target in the Code plan provides the next test of projectile travel and impact.
